Order flow does not tell you what price will do next with certainty, but it tells you where large participants are actively transacting — and that information is a genuine edge over reading price alone.

## Absorption

Absorption occurs when a large passive order repeatedly soaks up aggressive market orders at a single price level without that level breaking. Visually, price seems to "stick" to a level despite heavy volume trading through it. This is one of the clearest tells that a large player is defending a level rather than a lack of interest at that price.

## Iceberg Orders

An iceberg order shows only a small visible portion of a much larger order on the book, refilling as it gets filled. You can infer an iceberg is present when the same price level keeps absorbing volume far in excess of what the visible size should allow — the level "never runs out."

## Delta Divergence

When price makes a new high but cumulative delta (aggressive buying minus aggressive selling) fails to make a new high alongside it, that divergence often signals the move is being driven by passive limit orders rather than genuine aggressive demand — a classic precursor to a reversal.

## Putting It Together

No single order flow signal is a standalone trade trigger. The edge comes from combining absorption or iceberg behavior at a level that already matters (prior structure, a round number, a session high/low) — that confluence is what separates institutional footprint reading from noise-chasing.
