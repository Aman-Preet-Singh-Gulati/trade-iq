Tape reading predates every indicator on your chart — it's the practice of watching the raw stream of executed trades to build a real-time sense of who's in control, buyers or sellers.

## Speed and Size

The two variables that matter most on the tape are how *fast* trades are printing and how *large* they are. A sudden acceleration in print speed at a level, especially with larger-than-average size, usually means someone is willing to pay up (or sell down) aggressively to get filled — a genuine signal of urgency that a static chart pattern won't show you.

## Aggression vs Passivity

Trades printing at the ask are buyers paying up for immediacy; trades printing at the bid are sellers doing the same in the other direction. Watching which side is consistently paying for aggression, over a rolling window rather than a single print, gives a real-time read on short-term control that lags behind on a candlestick chart.

## Building the Skill

Tape reading is a pattern-recognition skill built through repetition, not something you can shortcut with a single rule. Start by watching the tape on a single liquid instrument for 30 minutes a day without trading — just narrating out loud what you see. The instinct compounds faster than most traders expect once you've logged real screen time.
