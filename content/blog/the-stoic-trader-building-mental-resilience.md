Every trader eventually discovers the same uncomfortable truth: the market does not care how you feel about your last trade. The traders who last are not the ones who never feel fear or greed — they are the ones who have built a process that functions regardless of how they feel.

## Decoupling Identity from P&L

The moment you start treating a losing trade as evidence about *who you are*, rather than one sample from a probabilistic process, you have introduced an emotional feedback loop that will degrade every decision that follows. A well-executed trade that loses money is still a well-executed trade. A poorly-executed trade that happens to win is still a mistake.

## The Stoic Reframe

Stoic philosophy offers a genuinely practical tool here: focus only on what is within your control. You control your entry criteria, your position size, your stop placement, and your adherence to your plan. You do not control what the market does next. Judging yourself on the outcome of a single trade, instead of on your adherence to process, is judging yourself on the one variable you never controlled in the first place.

## A Practical Resilience Routine

- **Pre-session:** review your rules, not your P&L. Walk in with your criteria fresh, not your prior result weighing on you.
- **In-session:** if you notice yourself sizing up "to get it back" after a loss, that is the signal to stop trading for the day, not to keep going.
- **Post-session:** journal the *decision quality*, separately from the *result*. Over enough trades, decision quality is what compounds — results are noisy in the short run by design.

Mental resilience is not the absence of emotion. It is a process robust enough that your emotions don't get a vote in your execution.
