"My stop always gets hit right before the market reverses" is one of the most common complaints among newer traders — and it is usually not bad luck. It's a direct consequence of predictable stop clustering.

## Where Retail Stops Cluster

Most retail traders place stops at obvious, round technical levels: just below a swing low, just above a round number, just outside a visible chart pattern. Because thousands of traders are looking at the same chart and drawing the same lines, stops cluster densely at these exact prices — creating a pool of liquidity that is genuinely attractive for larger players to trade into.

## It's Liquidity, Not Malice

A large buyer does not need to "hunt" your stop out of spite — they need liquidity to fill a large order without moving the market too far against themselves. A dense cluster of sell-stops just below a swing low is, mechanically, a pool of sell orders waiting to be triggered, which is exactly the liquidity a large buyer wants to absorb before pushing price higher.

## Placing Stops More Intelligently

1. **Add a buffer beyond the obvious level** — beyond the swing low itself, not exactly at it, so you're not sitting in the densest part of the cluster.
2. **Use volatility, not a fixed pip amount** — an ATR-based buffer adapts to current conditions instead of using the same distance in a calm market and a volatile one.
3. **Reduce size instead of tightening the stop** — if a wider, more sensible stop makes the position risk too large, the fix is a smaller position, not a stop placed exactly where everyone else's is.

## The Takeaway

You cannot eliminate stop hunts, but you can stop making yourself an easy, obvious target. Structuring stops around where liquidity actually clusters — rather than where a chart pattern "textbook-says" to place them — is a small adjustment with an outsized effect on your results.
