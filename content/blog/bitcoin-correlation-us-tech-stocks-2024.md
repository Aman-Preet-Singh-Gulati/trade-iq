Bitcoin was long marketed as an uncorrelated, non-sovereign store of value. Since 2022, its 90-day correlation to the Nasdaq 100 has spent extended periods above 0.6 — meaningfully undermining the "digital gold, uncorrelated to equities" narrative in the short-to-medium term.

## Why the Correlation Rose

The simplest explanation is also the most supported by the data: both Bitcoin and high-growth tech stocks are long-duration, liquidity-sensitive assets. When real yields rise and liquidity tightens, both get repriced lower for the same underlying reason — not because Bitcoin is "becoming a tech stock," but because both asset classes share the same primary macro sensitivity right now.

## When the Correlation Breaks Down

The correlation has historically weakened sharply around crypto-specific catalysts — exchange failures, regulatory decisions, or major protocol events — where Bitcoin moves on idiosyncratic news that has nothing to do with equity markets. This matters for portfolio construction: Bitcoin is not a reliable equity hedge *in a liquidity-driven selloff*, but it can still decouple meaningfully around crypto-specific events.

## Practical Implications

- **Don't rely on Bitcoin as a hedge during broad risk-off, liquidity-driven selloffs** — historically it has moved *with* equities in exactly those conditions, not against them.
- **Position sizing across a portfolio should account for the correlation regime**, not just each asset's standalone volatility — two "diversified" positions that are actually 0.7-correlated are not providing the diversification benefit the label suggests.
- **Watch real yields as a shared driver** for both asset classes — it's often a better leading indicator for Bitcoin's near-term direction than crypto-specific news flow.

The correlation is a regime, not a permanent structural fact — but trading Bitcoin as if it were still fully uncorrelated to equities has been a costly assumption over the past two years.
