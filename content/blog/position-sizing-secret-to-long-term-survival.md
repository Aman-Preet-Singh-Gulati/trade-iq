Ask a losing trader what went wrong and they will usually talk about their entry. Ask a professional risk manager and they will ask about position size first — because an oversized position turns an ordinary losing streak into an account-ending event, regardless of how good the entries were.

## The Math of Ruin

A trader risking 10% of their account per trade only needs a handful of consecutive losses to be functionally finished, even with a genuinely positive-expectancy strategy. A trader risking 1% per trade can survive the same losing streak with capital fully intact to trade the next setup. Strategy edge is irrelevant if you go broke before it has enough trades to play out.

## Fixed-Fractional Sizing

The simplest robust approach: risk a fixed percentage of current equity on every trade (commonly 0.5%–2%, depending on strategy volatility and conviction). Because the dollar amount at risk shrinks automatically during a drawdown and grows automatically during a winning streak, this method is naturally self-correcting — it de-risks you exactly when you're statistically more likely to be wrong.

## The Kelly Criterion, Used Carefully

The Kelly Criterion calculates the mathematically optimal fraction of capital to risk given your strategy's win rate and payoff ratio. Full Kelly sizing is famously aggressive and produces uncomfortable equity swings even when followed perfectly — most professional risk desks use a fraction of Kelly (often 25%–50%) specifically to trade off some theoretical growth rate for a much smoother, more survivable equity curve.

## The Takeaway

Position sizing is not the boring part of a trading plan — it is the part that determines whether you're still trading in two years. Get the entry roughly right and the sizing exactly right, and you'll outlast almost everyone who did it the other way around.
